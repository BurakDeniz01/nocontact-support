1. What the app is — and is not

No Contact is a motivational self-care companion for people who have chosen, for themselves, to take distance from a former partner after a breakup. It offers a day counter, an animated world, breathing and grounding exercises, a private journal, unsent letters, encouraging notifications and an optional on-device AI feature.

It is not:

  • a medical device, or medical, psychological, psychiatric or therapeutic advice, diagnosis or treatment;
  • a substitute for a doctor, psychologist, therapist, counsellor or any other qualified professional;
  • a crisis, emergency or suicide-prevention service;
  • monitored by any person. Nobody at No Contact can read your entries, see your mood, or notice that you are struggling.

If you are struggling with your mental health, please talk to a qualified professional. Asking for help is a strength, and the app encourages it.

2. Who may use the app

You must be at least 18 years old to use the app. The app is not directed at children or teenagers, and we ask parents and guardians to use the parental controls on their child's device.

3. "No contact" is your choice — and it is not right for every situation

The app supports a decision you have already made. It does not tell you what to do with your relationships, and nothing in it should be read as an instruction.

  • Do not use the app to avoid contact that you are legally or practically required to have — for example about shared children, a court order, shared housing, work or money. Follow the law and any professional advice you have been given.
  • The app is about distance from one person. It never asks you to withdraw from friends, family or anyone who supports you. Please stay close to them.
  • If you are afraid of a current or former partner, or have been threatened or harmed, contact the police or a domestic-violence service in your country. The app is not a safety tool.
  • Do not use the app, or anything written in it, to harass, threaten, stalk or harm another person.

4. Messages, notifications and milestones

The encouraging messages, milestone notes and notifications are general, pre-written texts. They are the same for everyone, they are scheduled in advance on your device, and they do not know how you feel, what happened today, or whether you have been in contact with anyone. They are encouragement, not advice, and not a promise about how or when you will feel better. Everyone's pace is different. You can turn notifications off at any time in Settings.

5. On-device AI ("Talk it through" and reflections)

On supported devices the app can use Apple's on-device language model to reflect on what you write. Please understand what that means:

  • You are talking to software, not a person and not a professional. The "Companion" is an AI.
  • AI-generated text can be wrong, incomplete, insensitive or inappropriate. Do not rely on it for decisions about your health, safety or relationships.
  • It runs on your device. We do not receive, read or review what you write or what the AI answers, so we cannot correct it or step in.
  • The app tries to recognise words that suggest self-harm or suicide and, when it does, stops the AI and shows support information instead. This check is a simple word list. It can miss things and it is not a safety guarantee. Never wait for the app to notice that you need help.

The AI features are optional. They run only when you open "Talk it through" or ask for a reflection, and everything else in the app works without them. You can also switch Apple Intelligence off in your iPhone's Settings.
